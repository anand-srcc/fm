What is the Time Value of Money?

Time Value of Money (TVM) is the principle that a rupee today is worth more than a rupee received in the future, because today's rupee can be invested to earn a return. TVM is the foundation of almost every valuation, budgeting and financing decision in corporate finance — including NPV, IRR, bond pricing, loan EMIs and retirement planning.

FV = PV × (1 + i)ⁿ PV = FV / (1 + i)ⁿ

Every TVM problem is built from five core inputs. Set any four and you can solve the fifth.

The Five TVM Variables

  • N — number of periods
  • I/Y — interest / discount rate per period
  • PV — present value (value today)
  • PMT — periodic payment (annuity)
  • FV — future value

Simple vs Compound Interest

Simple interest is earned only on the original principal. Compound interest is earned on principal and on previously earned interest — this is why compounding accelerates growth over time.

Simple FV = PV(1 + i·n) Compound FV = PV(1 + i)ⁿ

Cash-flow Timing

Ordinary annuity — payments at the end of each period (loan EMIs, bonds).

Annuity due — payments at the start of each period (rent, insurance premiums, SIPs paid in advance).

Why it matters

  • Prices bonds, shares and projects.
  • Drives EMI and loan design.
  • Underpins retirement and goal planning.
  • Connects directly to NPV and IRR.

Glossary

Discount rate

Required return used to translate future money into present value.

Compounding

Earning returns on principal plus previously earned returns.

Discount factor

1/(1+i)ⁿ, the multiplier that converts a future amount to PV.

Ordinary annuity

Equal payments at period-end.

Annuity due

Equal payments at period-start.

Perpetuity

A stream expected to continue indefinitely.

Nominal rate

Quoted annual rate before intra-year compounding.

EAR

True one-year compound rate.

YTM

Discount rate that equates a bond's promised cash flows to its market price.

Core Decision Rule

Always match the rate period to the cash-flow period. If payments are monthly, use a monthly rate and a monthly period count. Most TVM errors come from violating this rule.

Single Sum

=FV(rate,nper,0,-pv) =PV(rate,nper,0,-fv)

rate is the periodic rate; nper is the number of periods.

Annuity

=FV(rate,nper,-pmt,,type) =PV(rate,nper,-pmt,,type) =PMT(rate,nper,-pv,fv,type)

type = 0 for ordinary annuity, 1 for annuity due.

Effective Annual Rate

=EFFECT(nominal_rate,npery) =NOMINAL(effective_rate,npery)

Loan / EMI

=PMT(rate,nper,-loan_amount) =IPMT(rate,per,nper,-loan_amount) =PPMT(rate,per,nper,-loan_amount) =CUMIPMT(rate,nper,pv,start,end,type)

Solve for N or Rate

=NPER(rate,pmt,-pv,fv) =RATE(nper,pmt,-pv,fv)

How TVM Connects to NPV

NPV is simply the PV of an uneven cash-flow stream minus the initial investment. Every technique on this page — discounting a single sum, an annuity, a growing stream — is a building block used inside the NPV Lab's cash-flow discounting engine.

Learning Objectives

  • Distinguish simple from compound interest.
  • Compute FV/PV of a single sum, annuity and perpetuity.
  • Convert nominal rates to effective annual rates.

Classroom Activities

  1. Have students predict FV before calculating it, then compare.
  2. Change compounding frequency and discuss why EAR rises.
  3. Compare an ordinary annuity and annuity due with identical inputs.
  4. Build a real EMI schedule for a students' hypothetical home loan.
  5. Ask why NPV is "TVM applied to a project."

  1. Find the FV of ₹50,000 invested for 6 years at 9% compounded annually.
  2. Find the PV of ₹3,00,000 receivable in 7 years at a 12% discount rate.
  3. An investor deposits ₹8,000 at the end of each year for 15 years at 8%. Find the FV.
  4. A perpetuity pays ₹6,000 a year. At an 11% discount rate, find its PV.
  5. Compute the EMI on a ₹10,00,000 loan at 8.5% p.a. for 15 years, monthly payments.

Advanced / Case Questions
  1. A pension pays ₹1,00,000 next year, growing 6% annually for 25 years. Find its PV at a 10% discount rate.
  2. Compare the total interest paid on a 15-year vs a 20-year loan of the same principal and rate.
  3. An investment offers ₹40,000 in year 2, ₹60,000 in year 4 and ₹1,00,000 in year 7. Find its PV at 10%.
  4. Show algebraically why a growing perpetuity requires the discount rate to exceed the growth rate.
  5. A pension of ₹8,000 per month begins only after a 5-year deferral. At 9% p.a., find its present value today.
  6. An investor wants a ₹1 crore retirement corpus in 20 years with a ₹3,00,000 head start invested today. Find the required monthly SIP at 12% p.a.

Common Exam Mistakes

  • Mixing an annual rate with a monthly period count without converting either.
  • Forgetting to shift an annuity-due answer by one extra period of compounding.
  • Using a growing perpetuity formula when growth rate ≥ discount rate (formula breaks down).
  • Confusing total interest paid with the loan installment amount.
  • Rounding the periodic rate too early in a long amortization schedule.
